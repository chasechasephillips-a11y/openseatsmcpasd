Neighborhood post

Middleton / Cross Plains neighbors — tomorrow night, Tuesday September 22, is the MCPASD Annual Meeting at the Middleton High School Performing Arts Center, 2100 Bristol Street. We're doing a pizza tailgate outside at 6:30 and the meeting starts at 7:00. Everyone is welcome. There's a citizen petition on the agenda to open 4 of the district's 9 school board seats to any MCPASD resident. Today every seat is tied to a geographic area boundary set in 1963. The board stays at 9, every area keeps a guaranteed anchor seat, no current member loses anything, and voters still elect every seat. Here's the part most people don't know. The area that was the City of Middleton in 1963 holds four of the nine seats, so someone living there has a seat to run for almost every year, sometimes two in the same election. The areas with one seat get a turn once every three years. That means residents of roughly half the district are legally ineligible to run for school board two years out of every three. Not unlikely to win. Not allowed to file. Middleton had fewer than 5,000 people in 1963 and most of our neighborhoods hadn't been built. Those lines have never moved. Here's how the night goes: the district does its annual budget review first (about 10 to 15 minutes), then the petition comes to the floor. Anyone who disagrees gets to speak first, on purpose, before I speak. Then everyone present votes. Under Wisconsin law this is decided by the electors physically in the room. No absentee, no proxy. About 30 people attended last year. If you'd like a say either way, that's the room to be in. You don't need to bring anything (no photo ID required, you just sign in as an eligible elector), parking is free, and you should be home by about 7:45. openseatsmcpasd.org

Three rules for all of it

  1. Individual texts beat group texts. A group chat gets read; a one-on-one gets answered. Send five individual texts before you send one group text.
  2. Ask for a specific yes. "Come with me?" converts. "Let me know if you're interested" does not.
  3. Reminders aren't nagging. Most people who don't show up wanted to and forgot. The Tuesday 5:15 text is worth more than everything you sent last week.
