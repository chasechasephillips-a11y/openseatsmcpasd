Vote Yes MCPASD › Why now

Why again? The state stepped back.

Wisconsin decides how much our schools can spend. For years it hasn't kept up with costs, so districts like ours have to ask voters.

The cap

Since 1993, Wisconsin has capped how much each school district can collect from state aid and local property taxes combined. The cap used to rise with inflation. Since 2009 it hasn't. Today it grows $325 per student a year, far less than the cost of running schools.

MCPASD's cap for 2025-26 is about $112 million, roughly $16,000 per student. That includes the $24.9 million voters approved in 2022. If the cap had kept pace with inflation since 2009, MCPASD's would be about $23 million a year higher, roughly the size of this referendum.

Only voters can go over it

The school board can't raise the levy above the cap. If it tried, the state would take the excess back by cutting aid. The only way past the cap is a referendum. MCPASD voters said yes in 2018 (69%) and 2022 (63%). The 2022 approval ends after the 2026-27 school year.

State aid is flat, and our share is shrinking

The statewide general aid pot has been flat at $5.58 billion for three straight years, and about 60% of Wisconsin districts are getting less this year. MCPASD's preliminary 2026-27 budget shows its equalization aid down about 15%, covering only about 13% of the budget. The final state numbers come out Oct 15.

Less aid doesn't mean the schools get less money. The total is still capped. It means local property taxes cover more of the same total. Same total. Less from Wisconsin. More from us.

Your assessment didn't give the schools a dime

Home values in parts of the district jumped in recent revaluations. When values rise, the tax rate falls, and the district collects the same capped amount. Your assessment changes your slice of the pie, not the size of the pie. The fair comparison is your home against the average: if yours rose faster than average, your share went up. If it rose slower, your share went down.

A careful district

MCPASD holds a Moody's Aa1 rating, had a clean independent audit, and plans about $5 million in cuts over the next four years even if the referendum passes. Its school tax rate is below the Dane County average. See the comparison.

So: vote yes, and push the state

A yes vote keeps class sizes and programs where they are for four years. It doesn't fix the state formula. That takes calls to legislators too. Vote yes now. Push the state to fix it for good.
