The 60-second version

One ends. One begins.

This is an operating referendum. It pays for the day-to-day running of our schools: teachers and staff, class sizes, and programs. The 2022 referendum runs out after the 2026-27 school year. This one picks up the next year. They never overlap.

What a yes keeps

  • Competitive class sizes
  • Current programs, in class and after school
  • Pay and benefits to recruit and keep great staff
  • Upkeep of the buildings we already have

What a no would cost

  • Cuts of nearly 20% of the operating budget
  • As many as 250 positions
  • Closing an elementary school has been discussed
  • Bigger classes, and a harder time keeping teachers

A careful district

  • Aa1 credit rating from Moody's
  • Clean independent audit
  • Plans about $5M in cuts even if this passes
  • The state's top rating, Significantly Exceeds Expectations

Why are we voting again? Wisconsin caps what each district can collect. Since 2009 that cap has grown far slower than costs, and general state aid is flat in the current state budget. Only voters can approve going above the cap. MCPASD voters said yes in 2018 (69%) and 2022 (63%). What will it cost me? Nothing more in year one. For later years, check your own home with the district's tax calculator on the district's referendum page.

Sign and outreach rules, in plain English

  • Yard signs are fine on private property with the owner's okay. Renters can post them too.
  • No signs in the road right-of-way: terraces, ditches, medians, or utility poles.
  • No signs in parks, on school grounds, or on other public land without permission.

Questions at the door

Straight answers.

Isn't this a new tax on top of the old one?

No. The 2022 referendum ends after 2026-27. This one starts the next year and takes its place. They never overlap.

It replaces, it doesn't stack.

Will my taxes go up?

Not in year one: it holds the same $24.9M already in place. After that, set amounts step up each year to $34.6M in 2030-31, then it ends. The district's tax calculator shows the amount for your home.

What happens if it fails?

Cuts of nearly 20% of the operating budget. As many as 250 positions could go, and closing a school has been discussed. That means bigger classes and a harder time keeping great teachers.

It's not a raise. It's keeping what we have.

Didn't we just vote on this?

Yes, in 2022, when 63% said yes. It was always a four-year approval. This renews it.

I don't have kids in school. Why should I care?

Strong schools hold up every home's value on the block. MCPASD earned the state's top rating, Significantly Exceeds Expectations, with its best score ever (84.4).

Isn't the district just spending too much?

MCPASD spends about $17,160 per student, within about 2% of the Dane County average. Moody's rates it Aa1, its audit was clean, and it plans about $5M in cuts even if this passes.

Belt-tightening, not a 20% gutting.

My assessment went up. Doesn't the district get more money?

No. The state caps the total the district can collect. When assessments rise, the tax rate drops and the district collects the same capped amount. Your assessment changes your slice, not the size of the pizza. Only voters can make the pizza bigger.

Your reassessment didn't give the schools a dime more.

Can't the school board just raise the levy?

No. State law bars going over the cap, and the state would take back any excess by cutting aid. Only voters can raise it, by referendum.

Why doesn't the state just fix this?

It should, and we should push it to. The cap grows $325 per student a year, far below inflation, and general state aid is flat in the current budget. Our kids can't wait for Madison to sort it out.

Vote yes now. Push the state to fix it for good.

Is it for new buildings or a pool?

No. It's an operating referendum for day-to-day costs: teachers, staff, class sizes, programs, and upkeep. New buildings need a separate vote.

Is this forever?

No. Four school years, 2027-28 through 2030-31. Then it ends and voters decide again.

I'm on a fixed income. Is there help?

Year one adds $0. Wisconsin's Homestead Credit and the lottery credit lower property taxes for many homeowners. Ask your clerk or a tax preparer.

Who's behind this site?

Volunteers: parents, retirees, and neighbors in the MCPASD Yes Committee. The school district can share facts, but it can't ask you to vote yes. That's our job.
