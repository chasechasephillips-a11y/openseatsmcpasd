What can I do?

Why the jump isn't the story

Your taxes are your slice of a pie. Each taxing body decides how big the pie is (its levy), then divides it by everyone's assessed value. When nearly every home in the City goes up about a third, the tax rate goes down by about a quarter to raise the same money.

So the house down the road that "only" went up 30%? It isn't getting a deal at your expense. Its owner is in the same boat. The fair comparison is your home against the whole City, not against the house next door.

One more wrinkle: homes rose about 35% while commercial property rose about 30%, as reported from the City's staff presentation. When homes outpace businesses, homeowners as a group carry a slightly bigger share of the City's pie.

So what can you actually do?

  1. Check your property record for mistakes. Square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, finished basement. Errors happen in a citywide revaluation. Look yourself up on AccessDane and call the City Assessor's Office (608-821-8350) if something's off.
  2. Know the 2026 appeal window has closed. The City's final Board of Review session was September 22. Next spring, watch for Open Book, when you can talk to the assessor informally, and then Board of Review. You must give notice before Board of Review to be heard.
  3. Watch the budgets. That's where the real money is. The levy (the size of the pie) is set by elected people at public budget hearings this fall: the City Council, Dane County, MATC, and the school board. That decides your December bill far more than your assessment does.
  4. Send this to a neighbor who's panicking. Most people who rose about 32% won't see a tax change from the revaluation itself. It's worth ten seconds to find out.
