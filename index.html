Middleton-Cross Plains electors voted to open 4 of the district's 9 school board seats to any qualified resident, keeping 5 anchored to their Areas. A rule set in 1963 was put to the people who live under it, for the first time ever — and they changed it.

Thank you for showing up.

One hundred and six people came to a school district annual meeting on a Tuesday night in September. Last year, about thirty did. That difference is the whole story.

Thank you to everyone who signed a sheet on their porch, carried a clipboard to a ballgame, hosted neighbors at a kitchen table, drove someone who couldn't drive themselves, brought a kid who sat through forty-five minutes of budget review, or simply gave up an evening to sit in a room and raise a hand.

And thank you sincerely to the forty-six people who voted no. You came, you listened, several of you spoke, and you made the case for your neighborhoods. A vote is only worth something when both sides show up — and you showed up. This community is better for the fact that you did.

This was never a campaign against anyone. It was an invitation. We're not changing who serves. We're widening who can.

How it happened

Memorial Day to a show of hands

Four months, start to finish. No PAC, no party, no staff, no money. Just parents and a lot of driveways.

◆
Memorial Day · May 25, 2026

It started with a question nobody could answer

Why can't a qualified neighbor run for school board? Not "why would they lose" — why are they not allowed on the ballot. The answer turned out to be a map drawn in 1963, when Middleton had fewer than five thousand people and most of our neighborhoods were farm fields. It had never been reviewed. It had never been voted on. Under Wisconsin law, the only way to change it was a citizen petition and a vote at the district's Annual Meeting.

◆
June — August 2026

The summer of the clipboard

Wisconsin doesn't count an online signature. Every name had to be collected on paper, in person, with a circulator's certification — so neighbors carried sheets to farmers markets, youth baseball games, the Pioneer Pointe pool, block parties, church coffee hours, National Night Out, and a great many front doors.

Circulators across all five Areas gave up evenings and weekends. People signed on hoods of cars and on porches in the rain. The legal threshold was 500 signatures.

◆
Filed · August 2026

605 signatures, filed with the district

More than a hundred past what the statute required, every one of them pen on paper, gathered by hand. The petition was filed with the district clerk and placed on the Annual Meeting agenda — which meant that for the first time since 1963, the question would actually be put to the electors.

★
Tuesday, September 22, 2026 · 7:00 PM

The room decided it — 60 to 46

Middleton High School Performing Arts Center. The district moved the meeting to a larger room because they expected a crowd, and the crowd came. Pizza outside at 6:30. A budget review first. Then the motion, a second, and an open floor where anyone who disagreed spoke first, on purpose.

Wisconsin allows no absentee ballot and no proxy at an Annual Meeting. Only the electors physically present could vote. One hundred and six of them did.

What's next

Nothing changes tomorrow. That's by design.

No current board member loses a seat and no term is shortened. Seats convert only as they reach their natural re-election.

The phase-in

APRIL 2027 Two Area seats reaching re-election convert to open seats. For the first time, neighbors who were never eligible can put their name on a ballot.

APRIL 2028 A third converts.

APRIL 2030 The fourth completes the transition — 5 Area anchors + 4 open = 9 seats, the same nine we started with.

Every Area keeps a permanent, guaranteed representative. Voters still elect every single seat. The only thing that changed is that your address no longer decides whether you're allowed to run.

📌 This site is staying up. We'll keep it online for at least the next several months as a record of how this happened — the case, the full FAQ, the timeline, and the coverage. If you're in another Wisconsin district wondering whether a citizen petition is really possible: it is, it took one summer, and everything we used is still here in the toolkit.

Democracy, on a Tuesday.

It didn't take a party, a PAC, or a single dollar of outside money. It took neighbors willing to knock on a door in July and sit in a folding chair in September. Whatever you think of the outcome, that's the part worth keeping.

Thank you for participating in your democracy.

A parent-led citizen petition to open 4 Middleton-Cross Plains school board seats to any district resident. Passed by a vote of the electors, 60–46, at the Annual Meeting on September 22, 2026.

Petition authorized under Wis. Stat. § 120.02(2)(a).
