## KQ1. Volunteer show-up rates, and how reminders, confirmation and event structure affect show-up and retention (objectives b and c)

### Takeaway
The best volunteer-specific evidence comes from Haenschen and Sister District field experiments. Personal contact (a call and/or text) beats email-only, both for getting RSVPs and for getting people to show up: +168% RSVPs and +177% attendance for personal recruitment, and +51% attendance for confirmation calls compared with an email confirmation. A call paired with a text was the only confirmation arm that produced significantly more attendees. Once an automated text reminder is in place (Mobilize's), adding a personal text showed no further gain. I found no rigorous baseline flake rate. The only number found, "40-55% show-up without reminders," is a vendor claim. Canvass-event guidance is practitioner-only and consistent: set up early, train briefly, pair new volunteers with experienced ones, and debrief.

### Cited Findings
**Show-up and confirmation experiments**
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Katherine Haenschen, "If you text them, they will come: volunteer recruitment, confirmation, and attendance," *Interest Groups & Advocacy* 11 (2022): 399-415. The paper reports two exploratory field experiments with a partner organization. Recruiting by SMS "increased the rate at which volunteers signed up and attended relative to an email reminder alone, while also reducing the rate at which individuals failed to follow through." For confirming people who had already signed up, texts paired with calls worked best. Sample sizes and point estimates were not in the excerpts. (One search summary named the journal as *Journal of Political Marketing*, but the Springer URL is the *Interest Groups & Advocacy* journal, so I have used that.) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41309-022-00157-0)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy; run with Haenschen, then at Virginia Tech]** Sister District Action Network, 2018 studies:
  - Personal contact raised RSVP rates by **168%** and attendance by **177%** compared with email alone.
  - Calling volunteers to confirm an RSVP raised attendance by **51%** compared with an email confirmation.
  - Calls with voicemail, call plus text, and text-only all beat email. In the confirmation study, **only the call + text arm produced significantly more attendees** than email.
  - SDAN itself cautions that both studies were **underpowered** and drew only on Sister District volunteers, so they cannot be generalized.
  - These are relative lifts, not absolute show-up rates. — [Sister District](https://sisterdistrict.com/research/personal-contact-works-better-than-just-email); [States Win write-up](https://stateswin.org/research/call-or-text-your-girlfriend-personal-contact-works-better-than-just-email-to-recruit-and-confirm-volunteers-to-attend-team-events/); [SDAN Recruitment Whitepaper (2020)](https://stateswin.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Recruitment-Whitepaper.pdf)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy RCT]** Sister District, 2021. For an April 29, 2021 event, the RSVP list was randomized. Volunteers who also got a **personal** text did not attend at higher rates than those who got only Mobilize's **automated** text reminder. Authors' conclusion: "the auto-generated Mobilize text message reminder is sufficient." The earlier 2018-2020 work found that only the text-only condition significantly beat the email-only control on attendance. — [Sister District, "You don't have to tell me twice"](https://sisterdistrict.com/b/research/text-message-reminders-work/); [SD research index](https://sisterdistrict.com/research/)
- **[Industry blog; original study not found]** A blog reports that, in a study with the Maryland Democratic Party, reminder texts cut volunteer no-shows by **10%** and reminder calls cut them by **19%**. I could not locate the underlying study (a direct search found nothing), so treat this as **unverified**. — [Stones' Phones blog](https://www.stonesphones.com/post/an-easy-way-to-make-sure-volunteers-show-up)
- **[Industry/vendor; marketing claim]** EZ Texting claims that SMS shift confirmations with day-before and morning-of reminders raise volunteer show-up from **40-55% to 75-85%**. This is not independent evidence, but it is the only "baseline flake rate" number I found. — [EZ Texting political use case](https://www.eztexting.com/use-cases/nonprofits/political-government)
- **[Peer-reviewed; health-care analog, Cochrane review]** A meta-analysis of 8 RCTs found that text reminders raised appointment attendance with a risk ratio of about **1.14** compared with no reminder. This is useful only as an analog for the size of a reminder effect. — [PubMed 24310741](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24310741/)
- **[Industry/nonprofit-sector guidance]** General nonprofit volunteer-management advice:
  - Track the no-show rate as confirmed shifts minus completed shifts, broken out by shift type.
  - Send several reminders. One suggested cadence is one week, three days, and the day before.
  - Pair email with text or phone, because email alone is easy to miss.
  - Make cancelling or swapping easy, so a slot can be refilled instead of sitting empty.
  - Schedule "floaters" or backups. — [VolunteerHub](https://volunteerhub.com/blog/when-volunteers-dont-show-up-the-real-cost-of-short-staffed-shifts); [Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/en/orgs/reducing-volunteer-no-shows); [VolunteerMatrix](https://volunteermatrix.com/guides/how-to-reduce-no-shows)

**Commitment devices (analog evidence from voter research)**
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Nickerson & Rogers (2010), *Psychological Science* 21(2): 194-199, N = 287,228, run in the 2008 presidential primary. A call that walked people through a concrete plan (when, where, how) raised turnout among those contacted by **4.1 points**, and by **9.1 points** in single-eligible-voter households. Multiple-voter households showed no effect. A standard encouragement call and a self-prediction question had no significant effect. This is evidence that **concrete planning beats asking "will you?"**, a principle practitioners apply to shift confirmations, though it was not tested on volunteers. — [Wharton PDF](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Do_You_Have_a_Voting_Plan.pdf); [APS abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797609359326/); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-increase-voter-turnout)

**Canvass event structure (practitioner guidance only)**
- **[Practitioner/advocacy]** Indivisible's MiniVAN canvass guide says to:
  - Have the launch location "completely set up and ready to go a minimum of 15 minutes before your event start time" to welcome early arrivals and help them with the app.
  - Physically visit the turf at least **48 hours** ahead to confirm the list returns the intended addresses.
  - Invite first-time canvassers to come during that early window. — [Indivisible MiniVAN guide](https://indivisible.org/resource/minivan/)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy]** Indivisible Howard County (MD) recommends that newer canvassers "start off in pairs," with one person talking and one taking notes. It gives debrief prompts ("What went well? What was difficult?"). — [Indivisible HoCo guide](https://indivisiblehocomd.org/action-teams/red2blue/a-guide-to-canvassing/)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy]** A typical event flow: practice the script in a short training, knock doors with a partner for about 2 hours, then come back for a debrief. — [Indivisible event listing (Progress Kentucky)](https://indivisible.org/events/defend-democracy-deep-canvassing-with-progress-kentucky/). Indivisible's voter-registration guide also has volunteers pair up and practice their asks before going out, followed by a debrief. — [Indivisible Voter Registration Guide](https://indivisible.org/resource/voter-registration-guide/)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy]** Sister District publishes a "Follow Up and Thank Volunteers" case study on retention after events. I saw only the title; the content was not retrieved. — [Sister District case study](https://sisterdistrict.com/library/case-study-follow-up-and-thank-volunteers); [States Win copy](https://stateswin.org/?p=63443)
- **[Peer-reviewed, via secondary summaries]** Hahrie Han, *How Organizations Develop Activists* (OUP 2014). Han argues that high-engagement chapters "organize" (build people's motivation, skills and capacity, and give them real responsibility) as well as "mobilize" (transactional asks to people who are already motivated). Chapters that combined both "recruited more activists and mobilized more people" than those using mobilizing alone. — [Commons Library review](https://commonslibrary.org/how-organizations-develop-activists/); [EA Forum notes](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/sNEw8yfjZv4rBbkXN/notes-on-how-organisations-develop-activitists-why-are-some); [OUP](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/how-organizations-develop-activists-9780199336777)

### Inferences
- For a 13-person committee, the evidence supports this confirmation protocol: every shift sign-up gets a **personal call plus a text** about 24-48 hours before, then one automated text on the day. Going further (a second personal text) likely adds little, per Sister District 2021.
- Make every sign-up a **specific shift with a start time, a location and a partner**, and ask a planning question ("Where are you coming from? Who are you riding with?"). This borrows the Nickerson & Rogers plan-making mechanism. It is an untested extrapolation to volunteers.
- With no trustworthy baseline flake rate, the committee should **measure its own** (confirmed minus completed shifts) on the first weekend (Sat-Sun Oct 24-25). It should then over-recruit for GOTV weekend (Oct 31-Nov 3) based on that observed rate, not a borrowed rule of thumb.
- Staging should be a **private home or private business**, not a school building, because of the Wisconsin public-resource limits under KQ5. Run a 15-20 minute training with a role-play, send new volunteers out paired with returning ones, and debrief and recognize people on return. This both raises completion rates and creates the "organizing" experience Han links to retention.
- Give returning volunteers real roles (shift captain, data return, turf cutting). Han's distinction suggests responsibility, not just more asks, is what retains people and turns them into recruiters.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or reputable practitioner **baseline flake rate** for political canvass shifts. The commonly cited "plan for about 50% no-shows" rule could not be sourced. Hahrie Han's book excerpts gave no show-up statistics.
- Haenschen (2022) effect sizes and sample sizes were not retrievable (full text not opened).
- I found no Analyst Institute public summary on volunteer show-up. MoveOn, Swing Left and re:power canvass-event guides did not surface in searches.
- Two potentially relevant studies were found only by title, with no findings retrieved: a Behavioural Insights Team field experiment on a personalized "I've booked you a place. Good luck" text for event attendance ([BIT](https://www.bi.team/publications/ive-booked-you-a-place-good-luck-a-field-experiment-applying-behavioural-science-to-improve-attendance-at-high-impact-recruitment-events/)), and "Invitation Phone Calls Increase Attendance at Civic Meetings" ([MSU IPPSR](https://ippsr.msu.edu/research/invitation-phone-calls-increase-attendance-civic-meetings-evidence-field-experiment)).
- I found no direct evidence on the effect of debriefs, celebrations or "canvass parties" on retention. The guidance is practitioner consensus only.

## KQ2. Which recruitment approaches produce the most new volunteers quickly (objective a)

### Takeaway
Across academic and practitioner sources, personal one-to-one asks through existing relationships (calls and texts from someone the recruit knows) clearly outperform mass email. Sister District measured +168% RSVPs and +177% attendance for personal contact compared with email. A 2024 Dutch party experiment reportedly found the same for sign-ups. Han's work and her 2016 APSR experiments show that a relational context makes people more likely to recruit others. I found no rigorous measured evidence on "bring-a-buddy," "ask three friends," PTO/parent networks or retirees specifically.

### Cited Findings
- **[Practitioner/advocacy experiment]** Personal contact compared with email-only for recruiting to events: RSVP +**168%**, attendance +**177%** (Sister District with Haenschen, 2018; underpowered). — [Sister District](https://sisterdistrict.com/research/personal-contact-works-better-than-just-email)
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Haenschen (2022): SMS recruitment raised sign-up **and** attendance compared with email alone. She calls for more research on recruitment "that builds a social context." — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41309-022-00157-0)
- **[Peer-reviewed; excerpt only]** "Get Out the Volunteer: Encouraging Diverse Political Party Activism," *Political Analysis* (2024, Netherlands). Per the search summary, personal calls raised volunteer sign-ups "far more than mass emails did." Effect sizes were not retrieved. This is a non-US sample. — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/get-out-the-volunteer-encouraging-diverse-political-party-activism/85F013FC61AE97E01FD2DECCE31233BA)
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Han (2016), "The Organizational Roots of Political Activism: Field Experiments on Creating a Relational Context," *APSR* 110(2): 296-307. In three field experiments, a relational organizational context raised the likelihood that targets would **sign petitions, recruit others, and attend meetings**. Magnitudes were not retrieved (paywalled). — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/46EDE94DFF1B659CA1DB2D29C078B8C3); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/apsrev/v110y2016i02p296-307_00.html); [SNF Agora](https://snfagora.jhu.edu/resources/the-organizational-roots-of-political-activism-field-experiments-on-creating-a-relational-context)
- **[Secondary review of peer-reviewed book]** In Han's high-engagement cases, most recruitment "used personal asks and strong ties, thus leveraging existing relationships," and the structure of the conversations let friends "discover and develop shared commitments." — [Commons Library review](https://commonslibrary.org/how-organizations-develop-activists/); [Commons Library on Han and relationships](https://commonslibrary.org/hahrie-han-on-how-relationships-improve-mobilisation/)
- **[Industry/vendor-reported, Analyst Institute partnership]** In a 2020 Analyst Institute and Community Change Action test with 8 affiliates, contacts reached by a **friend or family member** were **6.5x** more likely to sign a petition and **8x** more likely to opt in to an organizing event than those reached by stranger P2P texting. It ran on the Impactive platform, a vendor with an interest in the result. — [Impactive blog](https://www.impactive.io/blog/harnessing-relational-organizing-to-build-capacity)
- **[Industry/vendor]** NGP VAN recommends offering a range of volunteer roles to fit different schedules and skills, and recruiting with personal calls and personalized emails and texts. This is advice, not measured evidence. — [NGP VAN, "What is GOTV"](https://www.ngpvan.com/blog/what-is-gotv/)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy]** Indivisible publishes a recruitment guide for local groups. The document was found but its contents were not retrieved. — [Indivisible Recruitment Guide (2020)](https://indivisible.org/sites/default/files/resources/2020-05-29_organizing_training_indivisible_recruitment_guide.pdf)

### Inferences
- The fastest multiplier for 13 people is a **personal-ask sprint**. Each member calls or texts (not emails) a personal list of likely sympathizers, such as fellow parents in class or team group chats, neighbors, and retirees who used to work in the district, with a specific shift ask.
- Pair each ask with a "bring someone" request, and make the first shift paired and social. This is an untested application of the relational-context findings.
- Mass email and newsletter blasts should be used only to announce shifts. Conversion should happen by personal follow-up call or text, per Sister District, Haenschen and the Dutch study.
- Ask existing volunteers to recruit at the debrief, right after a good shift. Han (2016) finds relational context raises recruiting-others behavior. The timing advice is an inference.
- School staff may recruit only on personal time and through personal channels (see KQ5). PTO and booster parent networks are natural relational lists, but their official channels (PTO email lists, school-hosted groups) need a legal check before use (see KQ5 gaps).

### Gaps
- I found no measured evidence on "bring-a-buddy," "ask three friends" or snowflake ladders, nor on yields from retirees or PTO/parent networks specifically.
- Effect sizes for the Dutch *Political Analysis* study and Han's APSR experiments were not retrievable.
- I found no Analyst Institute public summary of volunteer-recruitment experiments.

## KQ3. Measured turnout effects of relational (friend-to-friend) contact, peer-to-peer texting, follow-up contacts and "thank you" messages (objectives d and e)

### Takeaway
- **Stranger SMS/P2P texts** have small effects: about **0.25-0.29 points** on average, at most about 0.4 points optimized. Messages that **only** urge early voting did not raise turnout at all; they just shifted timing.
- **Friend-to-friend** contact shows much larger estimates: a meta-analytic estimate of **2.5-3 points** from Vote Rev, and **8.3 points** (CI 1.2-15.3) from the 2018 Outvote RCT, though a 2020 replication found much smaller, non-significant effects.
- **Second contacts to people already reached** add turnout: about **1.2 points**, and larger among people who said they intended to vote.
- **Gratitude ("thank you for voting in the past") mailers** raised turnout about **2.4-3.1 points** in Panagopoulos's three experiments.

### Cited Findings
**Relational / friend-to-friend**
- **[Conf. paper, peer-reviewed: The Web Conference 2021]** Schein, Vafa, Sridhar, Veitch, Quinn, Moffet, Blei & Green, "Assessing the Effects of Friend-to-Friend Texting on Turnout in the 2018 US Midterm Elections." Outvote randomized part of its app. The complier average causal effect (CACE) was **8.3 points, 95% CI 1.2-15.3**, and Columbia described it as comparable to door-to-door canvassing. The authors flag attenuation from outcome mismeasurement and imprecision from non-compliance. — [Paper PDF](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~blei/papers/ScheinVafaSridharVeitchQuinnMoffetBleiGreen2021.pdf); [Columbia News](https://news.columbia.edu/node/2833); [Conference listing](https://aaronschein.owlstown.net/publications/4782-assessing-the-effects-of-friend-to-friend-texting-on-turnout-in-the-2018-u-s-midterm-elections)
  - **Conflict:** the 2020 follow-up on the same platform found **much smaller effects, with a CI that includes zero**. — [Schein 2020 follow-up PDF](https://aaronschein.owlstown.net/publications/5974.pdf). A former NextGen America director called the 2018 result "so far removed from any GOTV texting experiment results I've run." — [Campaigns & Elections](https://campaignsandelections.com/campaigntech/new-studies-show-the-impact-texting-can-have-on-voter-turnout)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy]** Vote Rev: "Our meta-analysis estimates that a GOTV contact from a friend increases a voter's chance of turning out by **2.5-3 percentage points**." They describe this as above phone, text and mail. The number of studies and methods were not retrieved. — [Vote Rev research FAQ](https://voterev.helpjuice.com/en_US/research-and-evidence-faq/questions-about-the-research-and-evidence-behind-vote-rev-and-vote-rev-action-fund-tactics)
- **[Practitioner, right-of-center org; not peer-reviewed]** Center for Campaign Innovation, Florida field test: voters texted by people they **personally knew** were **8.6 points** more likely to vote than control, while traditional stranger P2P texting produced "no measurable turnout effect." — [Center for Campaign Innovation](https://campaigninnovation.org/research/measuring-the-power-of-personal-connection-a-relational-organizing-field-test)
- **[News; unverified]** A secondary outlet reports that an Analyst Institute meta-analysis found friend and family outreach about **3x** as effective as a door knock. The underlying AI analysis is not public, and I attribute this to Mother Jones based on the search result only. — [Mother Jones, Oct 2020](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2020/10/relational-organizing/)

**Stranger SMS / P2P texting**
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Haenschen, Mann & DeMora (2026), "The Effects of SMS Mobilization, Message, and Timing on Voter Turnout," *Political Behavior*. A pre-registered experiment with about **7.1 million** voters (2018 midterm; women and likely voters of color) found:
  - Any SMS raised turnout **0.25 points** (p < 0.001).
  - Election Day-only messages: **0.28 points**. Election Day plus early voting: **0.32 points**. **Early in-person-only messages: 0.04 points, not significant.**
  - Optimized data, message and targeting: **0.43 points**.
  - Effects were weaker in battlegrounds.
  - Messages with social elements, efficacy appeals or voter information raised participation.
  - The paper cites the Green & Gerber (2019) meta-analytic SMS average of **0.29 points (95% CI 0.11-0.47)**.
  - Haenschen: "Just telling people to vote early didn't actually increase turnout — it just prompted the same number of voters to vote earlier." — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11109-026-10121-2); [Northeastern News, Aug 11 2026](https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/08/11/text-messages-get-out-the-vote/)
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Dale & Strauss (2009), 2006 US field experiment: text reminders raised the probability of voting by **3.0 points** ("noticeable reminder" theory). This is an early, large estimate from a period when texts were novel. — [Dale & Strauss 2009 PDF](https://drexel.edu/greatworks/Theme/Spring/~/media/Files/greatworks/SP11/WK7_4_Dale_2009.ashx)
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Malhotra, Michelson, Rogers & Valenzuela (2011), *American Politics Research* 39(4): 664-681. Regular voters respond most to texts in **low-salience** races, and occasional voters respond most in high-salience races. Point estimates were not retrieved. — [Harvard DASH PDF](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/10471523/Rogers-TextMessages.pdf?sequence=1); [HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/text-messages-mobilization-tools-conditional-effect-habitual-voting-and-election)
- **[Practitioner; non-randomized]** Tech for Campaigns, 2020: about 1.4M P2P texts in 27 states. Texted voters turned out **0.7 points** higher than comparable untexted voters (a difference-in-differences style comparison). The gain was concentrated in low-propensity voters, with no change among high-turnout voters. — [Tech for Campaigns](https://techforcampaigns.org/impact/2020-texting-analysis)
- **[Non-US]** A Danish study found one unsolicited text raised turnout by about **0.75 points**. Additional messages added nothing, and content did not matter. — [Mark Pack summary](https://markpack.org.uk/150899/text-messaging-gotv-field-experiment)

**Follow-up / second contact with people already reached**
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Michelson, García Bedolla & McConnell (2009), as summarized in later work: there were "relatively large positive effects of a second round mobilization attempt... beyond an initial successful call when the second call provides a reminder to vote." The effect was especially large among people reached on the first call **who said they intended to vote**. — [J-PAL-hosted paper (Gerber, Huber, Fang & Reardon)](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/when-does-increasing-mobilization-effort-increase-turnout-new-theory-and)
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Gerber, Huber, Fang & Reardon (*American Politics Research*, 2020), N = 139,153. Second-round reminder calls raised turnout **1.2 points** among those contacted in an earlier attempt. Offering a reminder during the first call did not add turnout. — [ISPS Yale](https://isps.yale.edu/research/publications/isps20-16); [J-PAL PDF](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/when-does-increasing-mobilization-effort-increase-turnout-new-theory-and)
- **[Practitioner summary of academic program; excerpt only]** In García Bedolla & Michelson's California Votes Initiative work, follow-up phone calls produced "up to 10 percentage point effects — more than double that of a one-call phone bank." — [LWV Bay Area event page](https://www.lwvbae.org/league-news/dr-lisa-garcia-bedolla-will-speak-june-6-at-annual-meeting/); [CVI report](https://agingout.issuelab.org/resource/new-experiments-in-minority-voter-mobilization-a-report-on-the-california-votes-initiative.html)
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Nickerson & Rogers (2010): a voting-plan script raised turnout **4.1 points**, and **9.1 points** in single-voter households. This suits a "make a plan" follow-up with supporters identified at the door. — [Wharton PDF](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Do_You_Have_a_Voting_Plan.pdf)

**"Thank you" / gratitude messages**
- **[Peer-reviewed]** Panagopoulos (2011), "Thank You for Voting: Gratitude Expression and Voter Mobilization," *Journal of Politics* 73(3): 707-717. Three randomized experiments sent **pre-election** postcards thanking people for voting in the past. Turnout rose compared with no-mail control by about **2.4 points (Staten Island)**, **2.5 points (New Jersey)** and **2.4-3.1 points (Georgia)**. The effect was distinct from social pressure and robust across low- and high-propensity voters. The gratitude effect was reportedly about **5x** a generic reminder postcard. Exact estimates come from secondary summaries (excerpt only). — [ISPS Yale abstract](https://isps.yale.edu/research/publications/isps11-017); [Fordham News](https://news.fordham.edu/?p=32194); [Greater Good (UC Berkeley)](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/the_emotions_that_make_you_decide_to_vote)

### Inferences
- **Highest-yield follow-up for this committee:**
  - A **second, personal contact** with every "Yes" voter identified at the door: a text or call within the last 3-5 days restating the vote date and their plan (Michelson et al.; Gerber et al.).
  - **Relational texts** from volunteers to their own friends, neighbors and parent contacts, which carry the largest point estimates even allowing for the 2020 shrinkage.
  - **Stranger P2P blasts** are cheap but worth only a fraction of a point.
- **Early-vote messaging:** messages that **only** push early voting do not add voters (Haenschen et al. 2026). Pair "vote early at the clerk's office Oct 20-Nov 1" with "or vote Nov 3 at your polling place." The value of early voting to the committee is **logistical**: supporters who vote early can be removed from the GOTV-weekend list, freeing the 13 volunteers' limited hours. This is an inference.
- **"Thank you" messages** sent **before** Nov 3 to supporters with a voting history ("Thank you for voting in past school elections...") have the best evidence. A post-election "thank you for voting" has no tested turnout effect for this election. Its value is volunteer and community goodwill.
- Note the different message types: gratitude and social-information framing outperformed bare reminders (Panagopoulos; Haenschen et al.).

### Gaps
- The Analyst Institute's relational meta-analysis is not public, so its effect sizes are unverified.
- A reported "Turnout Nation / Donald Green 13.2-point" relational result appeared only in a slide summary. I could not identify its source and excluded it as unverified.
- Point estimates for Malhotra et al. (2011) were not retrieved.
- I found no evidence on the turnout effect of **post-election** thank-you messages, or of thank-you texts (as opposed to mailers).
- I found no school-referendum-specific (low-salience, local) relational or text experiments. Malhotra et al. suggest regular voters respond more in low-salience races, but the Nov 3 general is high-salience.

## KQ4. Field-guide priorities for GOTV weekend (final four days and 72 hours) and how to use a fixed stock of literature (objectives e and f)

### Takeaway
GOTV guidance (all practitioner or industry) is consistent:
- In the final four days, stop persuasion and ID work. Contact only **identified supporters**, prioritizing **sporadic or medium-propensity** ones over people who vote in every election.
- Help supporters make a **voting plan**, re-knock or call anyone not reached, and drop literature for supporters not at home.

On literature, Green & Gerber-era cost tables show leaflets and door hangers work but are weaker than face-to-face contact: roughly **1 vote per 66-77 contacts** for leaflets and hangers, against about **1 per 12 conversations** for canvassing. Leftover literature is therefore best used as **reminder door hangers for supporters not home**, not as cold drops.

### Cited Findings
- **[Industry/vendor]** CallHub GOTV guides:

## KQ5. School-referendum-specific guidance: Wisconsin legal limits (district resources vs. private committee) and typical committee tactics

### Takeaway
In Wisconsin the district may only provide **factual information**. Advocacy must come from a private **referendum committee**. Staff may advocate only on personal time without district email, computers or facilities, and the board cannot advocate as a body. A school-district-level referendum committee must register with the filing officer (CF-1) once it exceeds **$10,000** in a calendar year, within 10 business days. Practitioner school-referendum guidance (Lifto) is the same as general GOTV: sort the electorate into Yes, No and Undecided, then turn out the Yes voters.

### Cited Findings
- **[Gov't]** Wisconsin Ethics Commission rules on referendum committees:
  - A referendum committee that makes or accepts contributions, makes disbursements or incurs obligations "in an aggregate amount in excess of **$10,000**" in a calendar year must file a registration statement. It is due "no later than the **10th business day**" after the first contribution exceeding that amount.
  - County, municipal and school-district-level committees register with the **CF-1** form with the appropriate filing officer.
  - After registering, the committee files periodic campaign finance reports.
  - The manual (updated Sept 2024) is issued under Wis. Stat. § 11.1304(3). — [Ethics Commission, "Campaign Finance Overview - Referendum Committees"](https://ethics.wi.gov/Resources/Campaign%20Finance%20Overview%20-%20Referendum%20Committees.pdf); [Legislature-hosted copy](https://docs-preview.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/register/2019/763a4/register/guidance_documents/ethics_commission_eth/public_notice_cf_overview_referendum_committees.pdf)
  - The Ethics Commission also has an **Attribution/Disclaimer Requirements** page for referenda; its contents were not retrieved. — [Ethics Commission Referenda page](https://ethics.wi.gov/Pages/CampaignFinance/Referenda.aspx)
- **[Gov't, district guidance]** Stoughton Area School District staff guidance (2024 referendum): district funds may pay for factual information but **cannot be used for "express advocacy"**, and district computers should not be used to advocate. — [Stoughton SD staff information](https://www.stoughton.k12.wi.us/our-district/operational-referendum-2024/our-district/operational-referendum-2024/staff-information)
- **[News, neighboring district]** Verona Area: during work hours staff may provide neutral information but may not work on campaigns, and advocacy is allowed on personal time. Consultants advised **against letting advocacy groups use school facilities** for meetings. — [Verona Press](https://www.veronapress.com/archives/advocacy-policies/article_579c7a31-6968-5ff8-99ba-75c852fc2a11.html)
- **[Practitioner/advocacy]** School boards cannot advocate for a "yes" vote in their capacity as board members, but members may participate individually.   - School districts and officials may not use "public funds" or resources to advocate for a referendum result, and "public funds" is construed broadly.
  - There is risk of criminal exposure under **Wis. Stat. § 946.12** (misconduct in public office).
  - The Attorney General has treated phrases like "vote for" and "defeat" as indicators of advocacy. — [Renning, Lewis & Lacy](https://law-rll.com/referendum-advocacy-what-can-school-districts-school-district-officials-do/)
- **[Gov't, statute]** Wis. Stat. ch. 11 includes § 11.1205, "Use of government materials by candidates." It is aimed at candidates; whether it applies to referendum staff activity was not confirmed. — [Wis. Stat. ch. 11 PDF](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/11.pdf)

- **[Industry/education press]** Don Lifto (Minnesota referendum consultant) says the core strategy hasn't changed in decades: "canvassing... basically trying to sort the universe into Yes voters and No voters, and those who are undecided," then turning out the Yes voters. ### Inferences

  - Stage canvasses at private homes, businesses or parks, not school buildings.
  - Use personal phones and personal email only. Recruit staff volunteers through personal channels and outside work hours, and keep any school-staff volunteers off district email, rosters, devices and time.
  - Do not use student-family contact lists obtained through the district.
  - Put the Ethics Commission attribution ("Paid for by...") on all printed literature and door hangers. The exact wording needs confirming.
