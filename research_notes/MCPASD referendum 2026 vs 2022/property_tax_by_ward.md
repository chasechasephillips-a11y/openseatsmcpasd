# Property taxes by voting ward: what homeowners saw, what they paid, how they voted

Analysis run Nov. 12, 2026. This is a separate note. It does not change the research paper.

## Bottom line

- **The 2026 assessment notices were scary, and they landed weeks before the vote.** A typical home's assessed value jumped **about 35% in the City of Middleton, about 78% in the Village of Cross Plains core and about 44% in the Town of Berry**, because those three revalued to full market value for 2026. Homes in the Town of Middleton, Town of Cross Plains, Springfield and Westport saw **no change**, and Madison homes rose about 4%.
- **The money people actually paid rose much less, and most in the rural towns.** From the December 2022 bill to the December 2025 bill (all taxes, after credits), a typical home paid:
  - about **$750–$970 more (+12–13%)** in the City of Middleton,
  - about **$1,000 more (+14%)** in the Town of Middleton,
  - about **$900 more (+18%)** in the Village of Cross Plains,
  - **$1,400–$2,900 more (+27–43%)** in Springfield, the Town of Cross Plains and Berry.
- **The December 2026 school tax should be about flat, or lower, almost everywhere, despite the notices.** For a typical City of Middleton home, the school part of the bill should land between **about $350 lower and $110 higher**, depending on the 2026-27 levy. In the Village it should land between **$215 lower and $30 higher**. The notices measured a change in the yardstick, not in the bill.
- **How it lines up with the vote.** Yes ran furthest below what each area's partisanship predicts where real bills had risen fastest since 2022 (weighted r ≈ −0.68 across 13 areas, −0.50 without Berry) and where the 2026 notices jumped most (r ≈ −0.43). Berry is the extreme case: bills up 43%, notices up 44%, Yes at 28.9%, almost 18 points below expectation.
- **What it does not explain.** Notices jumped by about the same amount in every City of Middleton ward (34–37%), so the revaluation can't explain why City Wards 5–8 slid most (−4.6 points). The Madison wards had no notice shock (+4%) but lost 5.6 points. And tax experience doesn't predict the *change* in Yes from 2022 (r ≈ −0.16). Taxes help explain the Village and the rural towns, not the City or Madison.

## What this means for 2030

1. **Get ahead of the revaluation calendar.** Three municipalities sent notices up 35–78% in the months before the vote. In the Village, Open Book opened Aug. 3 (a mid-September Board of Review is reported but unconfirmed); the City's Board of Review met Sept. 22. Before the next referendum, find out which municipalities plan revaluations and explain beforehand that a higher assessed value isn't a higher school bill.
2. **Talk in dollars on a real bill.** Lead with what a typical home actually paid in December 2025 and what it will pay in December 2026. In the City, that's a school line of about $4,000–$5,000 that is roughly flat, not a "+35%" notice.
3. **The rural towns need their own message.** Their bills really did rise 27–43% in three years. That's real money, not a paper effect. Acknowledge it directly.

## Method

- **Homes and wards.** All 18,076 parcels in the MCPASD school district came from Dane County's tax-parcel GIS layer. Each was placed in its 2026 voting ward using Dane County's ward boundary layer, then grouped into the county's reporting units.
- **Sample.** Up to 50 likely homes per reporting unit were drawn at random (seed 20261112; improvements of at least $60,000, total value under $2.5M). Only parcels AccessDane classifies as residential (G1) were kept: 464 homes. Madison homes came from the City of Madison assessor's open data instead, all 2,432 residential parcels in the district, because AccessDane doesn't carry Madison assessments.
- **What was read for each home** (AccessDane parcel pages):
  - assessed value for 2022–2026,
  - DOR's estimated fair market value for 2025,
  - net tax bills for 2022–2025 (taxes minus lottery and first-dollar credits, specials excluded),
  - the 2025 MCPASD school line and school levy tax credit.
  Pages were fetched at a low rate, and AccessDane's disallowed tax-detail pages were not used.
- **New construction.** Homes whose market value rose more than 60% from 2022 to 2025, or whose 2022 bill was under 60% of their 2025 bill, are left out of the bill-change figures.
- **December 2026 school tax estimate.** Each home's 2025 school tax (before the state school levy credit) is scaled by two factors:
  - **The home's change in market value.** This uses DOR's 2026 residential economic change for the municipality (City of Middleton +4%, Village +3%, Town of Middleton +8%, Madison +4%, Town of Cross Plains +4%, Springfield −2%, Berry −6%, Westport +9%), adjusted by how far the home's notice moved relative to its municipality's average.
  - **The change in the district's equalized tax rate.** In 2025-26 the rate was 8.62 per $1,000 ($101.15M levy on an $11.73B base). For 2026-27 it would be 7.75 with a flat levy on DOR's $13.06B base, or 8.36 with an 8% increase.
  The 2026-27 levy had not been found, so the range brackets it. The school levy tax credit for 2026 is unknown and is not included.

## Caveats

- These are ward averages. They can't show how any one homeowner voted.
- There are only 13 to 14 voting areas, and the correlations lean heavily on Berry and the Village. Treat them as patterns, not proof.
- Samples of 19–50 homes per area give medians with margins of a few points (see the intervals in the CSV). The rural town samples are the thinnest.
- In the four rural towns, most Madison wards and City Wards 14–18, the partisan measure covers the whole reporting unit, not only its MCPASD part.
- The Dec. 2026 estimate depends on the levy the board certified in late October 2026, which wasn't checked, and on DOR's market estimates.

## Corrections to earlier notes

- **The Village of Cross Plains did revalue for 2026.** All 75 sampled Village homes carry a 2026 valuation date. Typical notices rose about 74–78%, up from a 0.65 assessment ratio. Earlier notes said the late roll "fits a revaluation but does not prove one."
- **The Town of Berry revalued for 2026,** with typical notices up about 44%.
- **The Town of Middleton did not change assessments for 2026.** 112 of 121 sampled Town homes still carry their 2025 valuation date, and the median change was 0%. The "notices mailed the week of April 14" revaluation in earlier notes most likely refers to 2025, which matches the Town's jump from a 0.73 to a 0.97 assessment ratio that year.
