# Property taxes by voting ward: what homeowners saw, what they paid, how they voted

Analysis run Nov. 12, 2026. This is a separate note. It does not change the research paper.

## Bottom line

- **The 2026 assessment notices were scary, and they landed weeks before the vote.** A typical home's assessed value jumped **about 35% in the City of Middleton, about 78% in the Village of Cross Plains core and about 44% in the Town of Berry**, because those three revalued to full market value for 2026. Homes in the Town of Middleton, Town of Cross Plains, Springfield and Westport saw **no change**, and Madison homes rose about 4%.
- **The money people actually paid rose much less, and most in the rural towns.** From the December 2022 bill to the December 2025 bill (all taxes, after credits), a typical home paid:
  - about **$750–$970 more (+12–13%)** in the City of Middleton,
  - about **$1,000 more (+14%)** in the Town of Middleton,
  - about **$900 more (+18%)** in the Village of Cross Plains,
  - **$1,400–$2,900 more (+27–43%)** in Springfield, the Town of Cross Plains and Berry.
- **The December 2026 school tax should be about flat, or lower, almost everywhere, despite the notices.** For a typical City of Middleton home, the school part of the bill should land between **about $350 lower and $110 higher**, depending on the 2026-27 levy. In the Village it should land between **$215 lower and $30 higher**. The notices measured a change in the yardstick, not in the bill.
- **How it lines up with the vote.** Yes ran furthest below what each area's partisanship predicts where real bills had risen fastest since 2022 (weighted r ≈ −0.68 across 13 areas, −0.50 without Berry) and where the 2026 notices jumped most (r ≈ −0.43). Berry is the extreme case: bills up 43%, notices up 44%, Yes at 28.9%, almost 18 points below expectation.
- **What it does not explain.** Notices jumped by about the same amount in every City of Middleton ward (34–37%), so the revaluation can't explain why City Wards 5–8 slid most (−4.6 points). The Madison wards had no notice shock (+4%) but lost 5.6 points. And tax experience doesn't predict the *change* in Yes from 2022 (r ≈ −0.16). Taxes help explain the Village and the rural towns, not the City or Madison.

## What this means for 2030

1. **Get ahead of the revaluation calendar.** Three municipalities sent notices up 35–78% in the months before the vote. In the Village, Open Book opened Aug. 3 (a mid-September Board of Review is reported but unconfirmed); the City's Board of Review met Sept. 22. Before the next referendum, find out which municipalities plan revaluations and explain beforehand that a higher assessed value isn't a higher school bill.
2. **Talk in dollars on a real bill.** Lead with what a typical home actually paid in December 2025 and what it will pay in December 2026. In the City, that's a school line of about $4,000–$5,000 that is roughly flat, not a "+35%" notice.
3. **The rural towns need their own message.** Their bills really did rise 27–43% in three years. That's real money, not a paper effect. Acknowledge it directly.

## Method

- **Homes and wards.** All 18,076 parcels in the MCPASD school district came from Dane County's tax-parcel GIS layer. Each was placed in its 2026 voting ward using Dane County's ward boundary layer, then grouped into the county's reporting units.
- **Sample.** Up to 50 likely homes per reporting unit were drawn at random (seed 20261112; improvements of at least $60,000, total value under $2.5M). Only parcels AccessDane classifies as residential (G1) were kept: 464 homes. Madison homes came from the City of Madison assessor's open data instead, all 2,431 residential parcels in the district, because AccessDane doesn't carry Madison assessments.
- **What was read for each home** (AccessDane parcel pages):
  - assessed value for 2022–2026,
  - DOR's estimated fair market value for 2025,
  - net tax bills for 2022–2025 (taxes minus lottery and first-dollar credits, specials excluded),
  - the 2025 MCPASD school line and school levy tax credit.
  Pages were fetched at a low rate, and AccessDane's disallowed tax-detail pages were not used.
- **New construction.** Homes whose market value rose more than 60% from 2022 to 2025, or whose 2022 bill was under 60% of their 2025 bill, are left out of the bill-change figures.
- **December 2026 school tax estimate.** Each home's 2025 school tax (before the state school levy credit) is scaled by two factors:
  - **The home's change in market value.** This uses DOR's 2026 residential economic change for the municipality (City of Middleton +4%, Village +3%, Town of Middleton +8%, Madison +4%, Town of Cross Plains +4%, Springfield −2%, Berry −6%, Westport +9%), adjusted by how far the home's notice moved relative to its municipality's average.
  - **The change in the district's equalized tax rate.** In 2025-26 the rate was 8.62 per $1,000 ($101.15M levy on an $11.73B base). For 2026-27 it would be 7.75 with a flat levy on DOR's $13.06B base, or 8.36 with an 8% increase.
  The 2026-27 levy had not been found, so the range brackets it. The school levy tax credit for 2026 is unknown and is not included.

## Caveats

- These are ward averages. They can't show how any one homeowner voted.
- There are only 13 to 14 voting areas, and the correlations lean heavily on Berry and the Village. Treat them as patterns, not proof.
- Samples of 19–50 homes per area give medians with margins of a few points (see the intervals in the CSV). The rural town samples are the thinnest.
- In the four rural towns, most Madison wards and City Wards 14–18, the partisan measure covers the whole reporting unit, not only its MCPASD part.
- The Dec. 2026 estimate depends on the levy the board certified in late October 2026, which wasn't checked, and on DOR's market estimates.

## Corrections to earlier notes

- **The Village of Cross Plains did revalue for 2026.** All 75 sampled Village homes carry a 2026 valuation date. Typical notices rose about 74–78%, up from a 0.65 assessment ratio. Earlier notes said the late roll "fits a revaluation but does not prove one."
- **The Town of Berry revalued for 2026,** with typical notices up about 44%.
- **The Town of Middleton did not change assessments for 2026.** 112 of 121 sampled Town homes still carry their 2025 valuation date, and the median change was 0%. The "notices mailed the week of April 14" revaluation in earlier notes most likely refers to 2025, which matches the Town's jump from a 0.73 to a 0.97 assessment ratio that year.

## Addendum, Nov. 13: what the median home pays, and the four-year referendum cost

The numbers are in `property_tax_costs_by_ward.csv`; the script is `property_tax_scripts/costs.py`.

- **Homes in the district.** About **13,000 homes** (houses and condos) sit on the district's 18,076 parcels. That count is the likely-home parcels in each area multiplied by the share of sampled parcels that are residential (80–98%), plus Madison's 2,509 residential parcels. An apartment building counts once, so households outnumber homes.
- **Assessed value.** The median home's 2026 assessment rose about **$118K–$159K** in the City of Middleton, **$177K** in the Village core and **$235K** in Berry. It didn't change in the Town of Middleton, the Town of Cross Plains, Springfield or Westport, and rose about $23K in Madison.
- **The school share of the bill.** In Dec. 2025 the school line, after the state credit, was about **half of the bill in the City (50–51%)** and the Village (46%). It was **54–64% in the towns**, where municipal taxes are lower. The school tax alone rose an estimated $600–$780 in the City and $1,000–$1,600 in the towns from Dec. 2022 to Dec. 2025.
- **Four-year cost of the 2026 referendum for the median home**, Dec. 2027 to Dec. 2030 bills:
  - City of Middleton: about **$4,300–$5,400** in all, of which **$690–$860** is new money above today's $24.9M.
  - Village core: about $3,500 ($550 new).
  - Town of Middleton: about $7,700 ($1,230 new).
  - Westport: about $12,600 ($2,000 new).
  The estimate is the home's share of district market value times $24.9M, $27.9M, $31.1M and $34.6M, holding that share constant; it equals about $907 per $100,000 of value in all and $145 per $100,000 new. The district's own new-money figure was $120 per $100,000. The same median City homes paid about **$2,550–$3,190** toward the 2022 referendum on the Dec. 2023 to Dec. 2026 bills.
- **All homes together.** Counting every home, total property tax bills rose about **$5.2M a year** in the City of Middleton since 2022. The Town of Middleton is up about $2.5M, the Village about $1M and the four rural towns about $2.7M. These totals multiply the average home's increase by the number of homes and cover the whole bill, not only the school part.

## Addendum, Nov. 13: renters and apartment buildings

- **Renters.** About 4 in 10 households in the district rent. That's roughly **4,800 to 8,100 renter households**, and about three-quarters to four-fifths of them live in buildings with five or more units. The range exists because census tracts don't follow the district line: the low figure counts only tracts at least 90% inside the district, and the high figure splits the partial tracts by land area. Source: ACS 5-year, Table B25032, via Esri Living Atlas, "ACS Housing Units in Structure" tract layer.
- **Apartment buildings.**
  - Madison part of the district: **34 apartment buildings with 1,963 units**, median 56 units each (City of Madison assessor data, property use "Apartments").
  - City of Middleton: about 210 parcels worth $2.5M or more. That group mixes apartments and businesses, because county data doesn't separate them.
  - Village of Cross Plains: 12 parcels worth $2.5M or more.
- **The revaluation shifted tax share from big buildings to homes (corrected Nov. 13: from commercial buildings, not apartments; see the next addendum).** In the City of Middleton, assessed values rose 33.6% for homes but only 23.7% for parcels worth $2.5M or more (2025 values from the county assessor layer, 2026 from the tax-parcel layer). Homes' share of the City's assessed base went from 63.0% to 64.8%, and large buildings' share from 35.6% to 33.9%. At about $40M of City school levy, that moves roughly **$0.7M a year of school tax from large buildings onto homes** (estimate). The Village shows the same pattern: homes up 66%, large buildings up 34%.
- **What renters pay through rent.** Madison-part apartments paid a median **$2,943 per unit** in property tax for 2025, about $245 a month. About half of that is school tax, roughly $125 a month (estimate). If fully passed through to rent, the 2026 referendum costs a typical unit about **$1,600 over four years** (about $33 a month), of which about $250 is new money (about $5 a month).
- **State credit.** Renters can claim Wisconsin's school property tax credit: 20% of rent (25% if heat isn't included) counts as property tax, for a credit of up to $300 a year.

## Addendum, Nov. 13 (later): renters by ward, school share and the Democratic-ticket No vote

The numbers are in `renters_school_by_ward.csv`; the scripts are in `property_tax_scripts/`.

- **Renters, from the 2020 Census.** The district had about **19,700 housing units, 19,000 households and 7,900 renter households (42%)**. This uses DHC block-group tenure applied to each block's households, with blocks assigned to voting wards; wards are built from census blocks, so the match is clean. Apartment units, counted as each block's housing units minus its house and condo parcels, total about **7,600**.
- **Apartment share of housing:**
  - City ward groups: 34–54%.
  - Madison wards: 55%.
  - Village core: 28%.
  - Springfield: 15%.
  - Town of Middleton: about 1%.
  Block-group renter rates overstate renters in the Town of Middleton, where its block groups mix in apartments outside the town; the apartment-unit count is the better measure there. Apartments built since 2020 aren't counted.
- **Apartments vs. homes in the revaluation, a correction.** The named apartment complexes checked (14 in the City and Village, by owner name, AccessDane) were reassessed up a median **33%** for 2026, about the same as homes (+34%). Their Dec. 2022 to Dec. 2025 bills rose a median **12%**, also matching homes, and about **49%** of their bill is school tax (44% in the Village). So the 24% average for City parcels worth $2.5M or more reflects commercial property rising less, not apartments. The shift in tax share was from commercial buildings onto homes and apartments alike.
- **Renters vote less, not differently.**
  - Apartment-heavy areas vote Yes more often (weighted r ≈ +0.64), but apartment share doesn't predict Yes once partisanship is counted (r ≈ 0.03).
  - It doesn't predict the Democratic-ticket No share either (r ≈ 0.12), and it barely relates to skipping the question (r ≈ 0.15).
  - Apartment-heavy areas cast far fewer ballots per household: **about 1.1 in City Wards 5–8 and 14–18 and in Madison, against 1.7–2.0 in the Town of Middleton** (r ≈ −0.82). Smaller households explain part of that; lower turnout likely explains the rest.
- **School share of the bill, Dec. 2025.** It's about **50–51% in the City, 46% in the Village and 54–64% in the towns**. Rental buildings pay the same school rate, and their share is 1–2 points lower only because they don't get the lottery and first-dollar credits.
- **School tax increase, Dec. 2022 to Dec. 2025.**
  - City: **+17.7%** for every home, because the City didn't revalue in those years.
  - Town of Middleton: +15–21%.
  - Village: +26%.
  - Westport: +17%.
  - Springfield: +33%.
  - Town of Cross Plains: +35%.
  - Berry: +38%.
- **Democratic-ticket No share** (minimum share of Crowley/Evers voters who voted No, assuming every Republican-ticket voter voted No), 2026:
  - Town of Middleton Wards 5–8: 11%.
  - City: 19–28%.
  - Village: 27%.
  - Madison: 20%, up from 12% in 2022, the biggest jump.
  - Berry: **50%**.
  It tracks the school tax increase across 13 areas (weighted r ≈ +0.55) but not its change since 2022 (r ≈ −0.12).

## Addendum, Nov. 13 (evening): what predicts the vote, and where the kids live

The numbers are in `drivers_by_ward.csv` (19 reporting units with 100+ votes) and `school_age_children_by_ward.csv`; the scripts are `property_tax_scripts/deep.py` and `corr.py`. All correlations are weighted by referendum votes.

- **Party first.** The Democratic governor share alone explains about **64%** of the variation in Yes across the 19 units, at about +0.7 Yes points per Democratic point.
- **Then real bills.** Adding the median Dec. 2022 to Dec. 2025 bill increase raises the explained share to **93%** across the 12 areas with bill data: about −0.75 Yes points per point of bill growth. Without Berry it's 90% (−0.71). The school tax increase gives the same result (91%, −0.81). Partial correlations with Yes, holding party constant: bill increase **−0.85**, school tax increase −0.81.
- **Home value runs the other way.** Holding party constant, higher median home value goes with *more* Yes: about **+1.6 points per $100,000** (R² 0.64 → 0.76; partial r +0.57). The No pressure came from lower-value areas with fast bill growth, not from expensive neighborhoods.
- **The 2026 notice jump** has a partial r of −0.44, and it adds little once home value is in the model.
- **Turnout.** More ballots per household goes with more Yes than party predicts (partial r +0.49).
- **Families.** School-age children per household has a partial r of +0.28. It's small: about +0.25 Yes points per point of under-18 share. Kid-heavy units have fewer Democratic-ticket No votes (r −0.31) and skip the question less (r −0.39).
- **Seniors and skipping.** The share of residents 65+ is the strongest predictor of skipping the question (r +0.62; 0.75 in units fully inside the district), at about +0.3 roll-off points per point of seniors. Seniors' link to Yes after party is small (−0.17).
- **Apartments** show no independent link to Yes (partial r −0.10).
- **Units fully inside the district (n = 9)** point the same way: home value partial +0.68, notice −0.65, bill −0.82.
- **Where the school-age kids live (2020 Census).** Children under 18 come from block counts (total population minus 18+). They're split to ages 5–17 using each block group's age mix and assigned to 2026 wards: about **8,070** in all, and 10,350 under 18.
  - Largest counts: City Wards 9–13, 19, 22, 24 (~1,110); City Wards 5–8, 21 (~850); Village Wards 1–6 (~830); City Wards 1–4 (~820); Town of Middleton Wards 5–8 (~790); City Wards 14–18 (~740); Madison Ward 119 (~650).
  - Highest per household: Town of Middleton (60–74 per 100 households), Madison Ward 101 (73), Madison Wards 118–119 (55–57).
  - For current numbers, the district could share student counts by ward or attendance area.
- **Caveats.** The analysis covers only 12–19 areas, and the factors overlap: the rural towns have low Democratic shares and fast bill growth. Partisan shares in split units cover the whole unit. The demographics are from 2020.

## Addendum, Nov. 13 (late): does "30% of residents have a kid in the district" hold up?

- **2020 Census, MCPASD as a school-district geography** (DHC via Esri Living Atlas, "USA 2020 Census Housing Characteristics – School Geographies"):
  - Households: **18,697**. Households with children under 18: **5,533 (29.6%)**.
  - Owner households with children: 4,032 of 10,747 (37.5%). Renter households with children: 1,501 of 7,950 (18.9%).
  - Population: 43,581. Under 18: 10,088 (23%). Ages 5–17: about 7,900. Age 65+: 7,550 (17%).
- **Verdict.** The district's "about 30%" matches the share of households with any child under 18. A child *enrolled in MCPASD* is narrower: it leaves out households whose kids are all under 5, in private or home school, or open-enrolled elsewhere. Enrollment is about 7,100–7,250 (4K–12, including nonresident open-enrollment students), against about 7,900 resident 5–17-year-olds. That puts households with an enrolled student at roughly **21–24%** (estimate), and lower in 2026 as apartment households have grown. Among adults, about 31% live with a child under 18, and roughly 22–25% with an enrolled student. That's consistent with "parents are about 1 in 4 voters."
- **By ward** (`households_with_kids_by_ward.csv`). Estimated from block counts of children divided by each block group's children per household with kids; the ward estimates add up to 29.4% of households, which checks against the Census's 29.6%.
  - Highest shares: Town of Middleton (38–47%), Madison Wards 118, 101 and 119 (38–46%), Village core (33%), Springfield (32%).
  - City ward groups: 21–31%.
  - Senior and apartment wards are lowest: Madison 114 and 117 (7–8%).
  - The district can compute exact households with an enrolled student by ward from its own address records.
