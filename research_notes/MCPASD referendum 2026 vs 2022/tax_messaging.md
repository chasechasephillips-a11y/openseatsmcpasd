# MCPASD operating referendums, Nov. 8, 2022 vs. Nov. 3, 2026: tax context, district finances and public messaging

Labels used below:
- **[primary]**: a page or document I read in full (news article text, DPI data table, district slide deck, or municipal document).
- **[secondhand]**: known only from a search-engine summary of a page I could not open. Treat these with more caution.

All arithmetic was done in Python on the cited figures.

## Q1. The 2022 question: amounts, years, purpose, tax-impact claims (and the "$24.9M" vs. "$65.5M" conflict)

### Takeaway
Both figures are correct, but they measure different things. The 2022 ballot asked to exceed the revenue limit on a non-recurring basis for four years: $7.345M, $13.79M, $19.435M and $24.9M. That totals $65.47M, which media rounded to $65.5M. "$24.9 million" was the district's own headline label and is the fourth-year amount. It passed with 62.9%. The district's tax claim was $7, $53, $39 and $36 per $100,000 of assessed value in years 1 through 4.

### Inferences
- "$24.9M non-recurring" and "$65.5M" describe the same question. $24.9M is the year-4 ceiling, which the district used as its label. $65.47M is the four-year sum, which the press used.
- The 2022 tax figures look like **year-over-year changes**. They add to a cumulative $135 per $100K of assessed value by 2026-27. Three things support this reading:
  - The labels are "Year 1 … Year 4."
  - Channel 3000 calls them each year's increase.
  - A $7.345M first-year levy alone would cost about $73 per $100K of 2023 equalized value ($7.345M ÷ $9.997B), not $7.
- The year-1 figure probably netted out other levy changes. The deck does not say so, so this is a gap. See Q3.

## Q2. The 2026 question: amounts, years, tax-impact claims, failure scenario, and why it is larger than 2022's

### Takeaway
2026 was a four-year non-recurring question: $24.9M in 2027-28, $27.9M in 2028-29, $31.1M in 2029-30 and $34.6M in 2030-31, totaling $118.5M. It passed on Nov. 3, 2026 with about 60.1% in unofficial results.

The district's final tax framing was $0, $21, $40 and $59 per $100K in years 1 through 4, compared with today's referendum level. That is $0, $105, $200 and $295 on a $500K home, about $120 per $100K (or $600 on $500K) cumulatively over four years.

The district had circulated a different set of numbers in August: $44, $65, $64 and $64 per $100K, which add to $237. Outlets used both sets.

About $99.6M of the $118.5M simply continues the 2022 level for four more years. Only about $18.9M is new authority. That structure is the main reason the headline total is bigger than 2022's.

### Inferences
- **$21/$40/$59 are levels above the current $24.9M referendum, not year-over-year steps.** NBC15's "Increase from previous year" header and its "$600 more annually by the fourth year" sentence appear to be wrong.
  - The new authority above $24.9M is +$3.0M in 2028-29, +$6.2M in 2029-30 and +$9.7M in 2030-31.
  - Dividing those by $21, $40 and $59 per $100K gives equalized values of about $14.3B, $15.5B and $16.4B.
  - Those values imply about 7% a year growth from the 2025 DPI figure of $11.73B. That is consistent with recent growth (Q3).
  - If the figures were year-over-year steps, year 4 would be $120 per $100K above today. Paying for a $9.7M increment at that rate would require an equalized value of only about $8B, below today's base.
  - So year 4 is about $295 a year above today on a $500K home, and $600 is the four-year cumulative total.
  - The Cap Times' "$120 per $100,000 … over the next four years" is a correct cumulative figure. Placed next to Waunakee's "$210 per year" in the same story, it is easy to misread as an annual cost.
- **The August $44/$65/$64/$64 set cannot be reconciled with the referendum-only math.** Year 1 is $44 even though the referendum amount is flat that year. NBC15 later stressed that the final figures exclude the $325/pupil adjustment and valuation changes. That suggests the August set was a broader projection of the total school tax change. This is unconfirmed; see Gaps.
- **Why 2026's headline total is about 81% larger than 2022's ($118.5M vs. $65.47M):**
  - Mostly structure. Four years at the existing $24.9M is $99.6M, about 84% of the $118.5M. Only about $18.9M ($3.0M + $6.2M + $9.7M) is new above the current level.
  - 2022 ramped up from zero. Its average was about $16.4M a year; 2026 averages about $29.6M a year.
  - The year-over-year growth in referendum authority is 12.0%, 11.5% and 11.3%, not "about 3%." The Examiner's 3% may describe the increments as a share of the whole budget: about $3M a year is roughly 2.5–3% of a district levy of about $101M. That is my inference.
  - The district's stated drivers are the gap with inflation since 2010 ($3,100 per pupil in the 2022 deck vs. $3,600 in 2026), low state aid as a property-rich district, and costs rising faster than the $325/year revenue-limit increase. See Q4.
- **If the measure had failed, bills would likely have fallen.** "No added referendum tax in year one" is accurate relative to 2026-27 bills. But if the question had failed, the ~$24.9M would have dropped out of the 2027-28 levy limit. That is roughly $212 per $100K at the 2025 equalized value, or about $1,060 on a $500K home, and somewhat less at a higher 2027 value. The district did not frame it this way. This is my inference.

## Q3. What MCPASD tax bills actually did, 2021-22 to 2026-27 (levy, mill rate, equalized value, typical home), and whether the 2022 tax claims held up

## Q4. State funding context: revenue limits, the 400-year veto, special education, the 2025-27 budget, and how they shaped MCPASD's ask

### Takeaway
2022's ask came after two years of $0 per-pupil revenue-limit increases (2021-22 and 2022-23) and 4.7% CPI. The 2023-25 budget then added $325 per pupil a year, which Evers' partial veto extended through 2425; the veto was upheld in April 2025. The 2025-27 budget kept $325 a year with no general-aid increase, so in a low-aid district like MCPASD the increase falls mostly on the property-tax levy. ## Q6. How the 2022 campaign and messaging differed (Yes committee, opposition, survey, tax framing)

### Takeaway
2022 was a district-led campaign pitched against a no-state-funding backdrop: two years of $0 revenue-limit increases, 4.7% CPI, declining enrollment and $6.3M in cuts already made. The district's headline was its final-year "$24.9 million."

Its survey had 1,925 responses and tested support by dollar level: 64% at $20M, 58% at $24.9M and 52% at $30M. The tax framing was year-over-year per $100K of assessed value.

I found no evidence of a 2022 Yes committee or organized opposition. The vote was 62.9% yes, versus about 60.1% in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **2022 survey:** "Community-wide survey information sent to every household within our boundaries … 1,925 survey responses received." Five community and two staff engagement sessions were held "this winter." Support by proposed amount: $20M 64%, $24.9M 58%, $30M 52%. — [2022 deck, slides 22–23](https://clubrunner.blob.core.windows.net/00000006121/en-ca/files/homepage/m-cp-school-district-2022-referendum/Nov-2022-Referendum-M-CP-area-school-district-Presentation.pdf)
- Monogue told NBC15 the district "surveyed 17 thousand residents … which resulted in positive feedback." — [WMTV, Nov. 3, 2022](https://www.wmtv15news.com/2022/11/03/schools-ballot-breaking-down-district-referenda/)
- [secondhand] There were five fall community conversations, two in person and three virtual. — [MCPASD "Join the Conversation" (search summary)](https://www.mcpasd.k12.wi.us/article/830532)
- **Budget backdrop the district presented in 2022:**
  - Deficit-budget reductions of $2,269,000 in 2021-22 and $4,070,000 in 2022-23, totaling $6,339,000
  - About $6.9M in one-time COVID relief
  - Declining enrollment
  - Wisconsin's per-pupil spending rank falling from 11th (2002) to 25th (2020)
  - The K-12 share of state general-purpose revenue falling from 38% (2011) to 31% (2021)
  - The district's referendum history: 24 questions, 11 passed; the last was in 2018 ($4.8M operating, with more than 70% yes)
  - Source: [2022 deck, slides 5, 6, 16, 17, 18](https://clubrunner.blob.core.windows.net/00000006121/en-ca/files/homepage/m-cp-school-district-2022-referendum/Nov-2022-Referendum-M-CP-area-school-district-Presentation.pdf)
- The 2018 pair (a $138.9M bond and a $4.8M operating question) passed with 69.6% and 69.2%. — [Channel 3000, Nov. 9, 2018](https://www.channel3000.com/news/local-news/largest-school-referendum-in-the-state-passes-in-middleton/article_3d70481f-fba4-56ed-ad87-923a4783d0cf.html)
- **2022 framing in the press:** the Cap Times placed MCPASD among eight Dane County districts seeking a combined "$75.5 million in non-recurring operating referendums." It stressed that the asks came as districts held one-time COVID money they said could not responsibly fund ongoing costs. — [Cap Times, Oct. 24, 2022](https://captimes.com/news/education/eight-dane-county-school-districts-have-referenda-on-november-ballot/article_ce8ceb2d-51d4-55cb-a08b-47b3c7665771.html)
- **Another tax question on the same 2022 ballot:** the City of Middleton put its first-ever operating referendum before voters on Nov. 8, 2022. It would add $770,000 to the city levy, or $19.79 per $100,000 of assessed value, for two police officers, a communications specialist, a parks worker and a 4% non-union raise. — [Channel 3000, Sept. 27, 2022](https://www.channel3000.com/news/local-news/middleton-referendum-asks-residents-to-approve-tax-increase-to-address-staffing-shortage/article_82196888-e88e-5a21-924f-78d47be393dd.html)
- Hesselbein in 2026 on the 2022 framing: the board "ran out of ways to cut things" four years ago, and the four-year term was chosen in the hope that "sensibility would return from the state." — [Cap Times, Oct. 20, 2026](https://captimes.com/news/education/two-dane-county-school-districts-ask-voters-to-raise-property-taxes/article_56b581ca-9603-48fd-ad2e-566c6748b5bc.html)

### Inferences
- **Differences between the cycles:**
  - *Cuts:* 2022 used a ramp ($7M to $25M) and cited prior cuts. 2026 used a flat renewal plus inflation step-ups, with $5M in cuts promised even if it passed.
  - *Survey design:* the 2022 survey tested support by dollar level. The 2026 survey tested three four-year options and was weighted to likely voters (secondhand).
  - *Tax figures:* 2022 published year-over-year steps. 2026 published levels above the current referendum, plus a cumulative four-year total that media widely reported.
  - *Campaign:* in 2026 an independent Yes campaign (Tawil) did visible field work; I found no equivalent for 2022.
- The 2022 survey's "58% at $24.9M" is roughly in line with the 62.9% result. The 2026 unweighted 66% overstated the 60.1% result, and the weighted ~47% understated it.
- Monogue's "17 thousand residents" likely refers to households mailed, not responses (the deck reports 1,925 responses). This is my inference.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence on whether a 2022 Yes committee, PAC or organized opposition existed, and no 2022 letters to the editor.
- I did not find the 2022 results of the City of Middleton operating referendum or how the two 2022 questions interacted.
- I found no 2022 coverage in the Times-Tribune or State Journal that framed the tax impact differently.

## Q7. Did property revaluations or rising housing costs shape tax sensitivity in 2026?

### Takeaway
Both the City of Middleton and the Town of Middleton carried out revaluations for the 2026 assessment year. Town notices were mailed in April 2026. City notices came in summer 2026, with Board of Review in September, weeks before the vote. The City had been assessing at only 78% of market, so large jumps on notices were likely. Officials stressed that revaluation alone does not raise taxes.

I found no news reporting that directly tied revaluations to referendum sentiment. Housing cost appeared mostly as "average $500,000 home" framing, and in 2022 as a cause of enrollment decline.

### Cited Findings
- **City of Middleton [primary]:** the city "contracted with Associated Appraisal Consultants … to perform a revaluation of all property for the 2026 assessment cycle … to bring all assessed values to 100% market value." Exterior inspections began in November 2025. — [City of Middleton Revaluation Overview](https://www.cityofmiddleton.us/DocumentCenter/View/20472/Middleton-Revaluation-Overview)
- **City's 2025 assessment ratio [primary]:** 0.7827 — [Dane County Treasurer, 2025 mill rates](https://treasurer.danecounty.gov/documents/pdf/CurrentYear-DaneCountyMillRatesByDistrict-NOT-C-Madison.pdf). Reaching 100% implies about a +28% average assessment increase (my arithmetic).
- **City's 2026 notice [secondhand]:** assessed values reflect Jan. 1, 2026 market conditions. Total assessed value rose about 32%, and individual properties may differ. Revaluation "redistributes how the tax levy is shared among properties; it does not, by itself, generate additional tax revenue." Board of Review was Sept. 22, 2026. — [City of Middleton, "Your 2026 Property Assessment: What You Need to Know" (search summary; page 404'd on curl)](https://cityofmiddleton.us/CivicSend/ViewMessage/message/297551)
- **Town of Middleton [primary]:** "The Town Assessor has completed a revaluation of the Town … new assessment notices will be mailed out the week of April 14th." Open Book was April 23 and Board of Review May 12, 2026. — [Town of Middleton Spring 2026 newsletter](https://middletonwi.govoffice3.com/vertical/Sites/%7B97A50AAB-3824-4833-ACEA-EF2B9A14C856%7D/uploads/Spring_2026_website.pdf); [Town property tax page](https://town.middleton.wi.us/propertytax). The Town's 2025 assessment ratio was 0.9659 — [Dane County Treasurer](https://treasurer.danecounty.gov/documents/pdf/CurrentYear-DaneCountyMillRatesByDistrict-NOT-C-Madison.pdf)
- **A third-party sample alert (illustrative):** BadgerHomeowner's "sample email" shows a Middleton home "Up 31% in 2026 from $467,900. The median Middleton home rose 32%." It is a marketing sample, not reporting. — [BadgerHomeowner](https://www.badgerhomeowner.com/school-referendums-dane-county/)
- **Valuation changes left out of the district's tax figures:** NBC15 noted the district's figures exclude "property valuation changes." — [WMTV, Oct. 22, 2026](https://www.wmtv15news.com/2026/10/22/middleton-cross-plains-voters-weigh-school-referendum-district-faces-nearly-25m-potential-cuts/)
- **Housing cost framing:**
  - "The average cost of a house is $500,000 in Middleton." The skeptical voter quoted was a realtor (Murrain). — [Wisconsin Examiner via Civic Media](https://civicmedia.us/news/2026/09/21/middleton-referendum-discussion-highlights-tension-between-property-taxes-and-school-funding)
  - In 2022 the district blamed the housing market for enrollment decline ("Price-points in our community are too high for many families with young children"). — [2022 deck, slide 12](https://clubrunner.blob.core.windows.net/00000006121/en-ca/files/homepage/m-cp-school-district-2022-referendum/Nov-2022-Referendum-M-CP-area-school-district-Presentation.pdf)
- **District-wide values:** TIF-out equalized value grew 7.0% in 2025 after 9–15% annual gains from 2022 to 2024. — [DPI FY2025-26](https://sfs.dpi.wi.gov/safr_ro/all_mill_rate.asp?year=2026); [DPI FY2023-24](https://sfs.dpi.wi.gov/safr_ro/all_mill_rate.asp?year=2024)

### Inferences
- City of Middleton owners received notices showing roughly 28–32% assessment jumps in summer 2026, during the campaign. This is a reassessment effect, not a tax increase. Tax shifts among properties depend on whether a home rose more or less than average. Even so, it plausibly heightened tax sensitivity. I have no direct evidence of this.
- Because the district quoted impacts per $100K of fair market value, not assessed value, the revaluation did not change the referendum math. It may still have made "per $100K" figures feel abstract next to a fresh, higher assessment, which the "Who has a $100,000 house?" exchange illustrates.

### Gaps
- I found no article, letter or survey result linking the 2026 City or Town revaluations to referendum attitudes.
- I did not check whether the Village of Cross Plains, Town of Cross Plains, Town of Springfield, Town of Berry or the Madison and Verona portions of the district revalued in 2026.
- 2026 municipal tax rates and bills, which would show the actual 2026-27 school portion after revaluation, were not available. The City said rates are set after fall budgets.
